# 08 — The Capacity Conversation

**This is the highest-value document in the system.** Not a tracker — a conversation.

You can't decline pursuits; they're assigned. So triage isn't available to you, and no
spreadsheet will fix an assignment problem. What *is* available is making the tradeoff
visible and letting leadership choose it.

---

## The one thing leadership doesn't know

From above, **"Sam has five people"** reads as *Sam has capacity.*

Nobody has connected that **all five are consumed by the Galleria pursuit**, which means
your other 15+ pursuits have **zero support**. You are running that entire pipeline
solo, while also overseeing the Galleria team.

That is invisible from where your boss sits. And invisible problems never get solved —
they just get absorbed by whoever is absorbing them, until that person breaks or leaves.

**Making it visible is the whole job.** Not complaining. Reporting.

---

## The critical reframe: don't ask to decline — ask to rank

This is the difference between a conversation that works and one that damages you.

| ❌ Don't say | ✅ Say |
|---|---|
| "I have too much work" | "Here's the capacity math — which do you want me to win?" |
| "Can I drop some of these?" | "Which five are the priority? I'll resource those properly." |
| "I'm overwhelmed" | "At current staffing, 15 pursuits means thin bids on all 15." |

A boss will rarely say *"drop it."* Declining costs them something politically. But a
boss will very readily say *"Galleria, and these two, are what matter."*

**That gets you the same outcome with none of the political cost** — and it moves the
decision to the person who's actually authorized to make it. You stop being the person
who wants less work and become the person managing a portfolio.

You also get cover. If the answer is "do all fifteen," you've documented that you raised
it — and when a thin bid loses, the conversation is about resourcing, not about you.

---

## The memo

Verify every number before sending. These are structural placeholders, not facts.

> **Subject: Precon capacity — pursuit pipeline vs. current staffing**
>
> Wanted to give you a clear picture of where precon capacity stands, and get your
> direction on priorities.
>
> **Current load**
> - Active pursuits assigned to me: **15+**
> - Precon staff: **5 estimators — all currently committed to the Galleria Mall pursuit**
> - Support available for the other 15 pursuits: **none**
> - I'm also covering delivery responsibilities on active work
>
> **What that means in hours**
> A pursuit needs roughly 2 hours a week of senior attention to stay current — sub
> solicitation and follow-up, RFI and addendum review, scope gaps, pricing decisions.
> Fifteen pursuits is **30+ hours a week** before any delivery work, any presentation
> development, or any oversight of the Galleria team.
>
> **The risk**
> This doesn't fail loudly. It fails as thin coverage — trades with one unconfirmed
> bidder, generic presentations, missed addenda — and it shows up in hit rate a quarter
> later. I'd rather flag it now than explain it then.
>
> **What I'm asking for**
> Not to drop anything. Just your ranking:
>
> 1. **Which 5 pursuits do we most want to win?** I'll resource those properly —
>    full sub coverage, custom presentations, real strategy.
> 2. The remainder I'll cover at a standard level, with the understanding that they're
>    receiving proportionally less.
>
> Alternatively, if all 15 need full treatment, I'd want to talk about adding a
> resource or pulling someone off Galleria.
>
> Happy to walk through the pursuit list whenever works.

---

## Why this memo works

1. **It's numbers, not feelings.** "30+ hours" is a fact leadership can act on.
   "I'm swamped" is a mood they can dismiss.
2. **It offers solutions, not just problems.** Three options, all reasonable.
3. **It names the business risk in their language** — hit rate, not workload.
4. **It doesn't ask permission to do less.** It asks for prioritization, which is
   literally your boss's job.
5. **It protects you.** If nothing changes, you raised it in writing, in good faith,
   with a proposed fix.

## Before you send it

- **Verify the numbers.** Count the actual pursuits. Check whether 2 hrs/week is right
  for your work — if it's 3, the case is stronger, and if it's 1, say 1.
- **Have your five picks ready.** If asked "which would you choose," a fast confident
  answer makes you look like a portfolio manager. Hesitating undoes the whole memo.
- **Know Galleria's timeline.** "The team frees up after X" changes the conversation
  from a staffing request into a scheduling one, which is far easier to say yes to.
- **Pick the moment.** Not during a crisis, not right after a loss. A regular 1:1 is
  ideal.

---

## If the answer is "do all fifteen"

Then you have real information, and three moves:

1. **Standard-treatment everything.** No custom decks below the top five. Consistency
   over polish. Protect the top five absolutely.
2. **Coverage as the only metric you track.** One number per pursuit: uncovered trades.
   Nothing else. It's what actually loses bids.
3. **Revisit when Galleria resolves.** Win or lose, five estimators come free. Have
   your ask ready for that exact day — that's your leverage moment, and it's coming.

And a longer-term read worth being honest with yourself about: if the assignment stays
impossible after you've made it visible with numbers and a proposed fix, that's data
about the role, not about you. You support six people on this income. Knowing whether
the job is structurally sound matters more than any tracker.

## The lightweight tracker — because everything changes hourly

You said it all changes constantly. So anything that takes 20 minutes a week to maintain
will be abandoned by week three. **Rule: if updating it takes more than 30 seconds, it's
too heavy.**

One line per pursuit. Nothing else:

```
GALLERIA      | Tier 1 | due 8/15 | subs: 12/14 covered | ⚠ MEP uncovered
24TH AVE      | Tier 2 | due 8/22 | subs:  6/9  covered | ⚠ glazing, roofing
193 MARKET    | Tier 2 | due 9/05 | subs:  8/8  covered | ok
```

- **Coverage is the only status that matters.** Not "in progress" — that tells you
  nothing. Uncovered trades is what loses bids.
- **Update it when something changes, not on a schedule.** Schedules die; triggers don't.
- Keep it one screen. If it doesn't fit on your phone, it's too long.
