# 05 — Pursuit Tracker (Work)

Hudson Meridian. Mixed precon + delivery role. **15+ active pursuits.** Five estimators,
junior — they execute well but don't yet decide.

**Status: manual until IT connects `samin@hudsonmeridian.com`.**

---

## Start here: this is a capacity problem, not a filing problem

At 2 hours per pursuit per week — status, sub decisions, pricing calls — 15+ pursuits is
**30+ hours** before any delivery work, any presentation, or any managing. It does not
fit in a week. It was never going to.

So a tracker alone won't help. It'll just give you a tidier view of an impossible load.
Two things actually change the math:

1. **Triage** — stop treating 15 pursuits as if they deserve equal effort
2. **Standards** — make junior output predictable so you check instead of redo

Everything below serves those two.

> **The uncomfortable one:** with a junior team, the way to win *more* work is usually
> to pursue *less* of it. Fifteen thin bids with rushed pricing and a generic deck lose
> to five well-resourced ones. If your hit rate is dropping, volume is the likeliest
> cause — and it's the only variable you fully control.

---

## A. Triage — do this first, before any tracking

Sort all 15+ into three tiers. Be honest, and be harsh.

| Tier | Count | What it gets |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Must win** | 3–5 max | Your personal attention. Custom presentation. Full sub coverage. Real strategy. |
| **2 — Should bid** | 5–7 | Standard process, team-led. You review at two checkpoints only. |
| **3 — Coverage** | rest | Minimum viable bid, or **decline**. No custom work. No evenings. |

### Go / no-go scoring

Score each pursuit 1–5. Anything under 15 is Tier 3 or a decline.

| Factor | Question |
|---|---|
| **Relationship** | Do we know this client, or are we a name on a list? |
| **Fit** | Is this our building type, size, and delivery method? |
| **Competition** | How many bidders? Under 4 is real; over 8 is a lottery. |
| **Resources** | Do we actually have people for this, at this time? |
| **Strategic value** | Does winning open a door beyond this one job? |

> **Declining is a skill.** A fast, gracious no-bid protects the relationship better than
> a thin bid that shows you didn't take it seriously. Clients remember both.

Tier 3 pursuits are where your evenings are going. Cut them first.

## C. Sub packages — delegate this first

This is the single most delegable thing you do, and the one where a junior with a
checklist performs as well as you. **Hand it over completely.**

| Project | Trade | Sub / vendor | Sent | Due back | Confirmed | Received | Chased | Coverage |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 193 Market St | | Everest (`eri@everest.al`) | | | | | | |

### The chase protocol — give this to your team verbatim

| When | Action |
|---|---|
| **Day 0** | Send package. Log it. |
| **Day 1–2** | **Confirm receipt by phone.** Not email. A voice confirms; silence doesn't. |
| **50% of window** | First chase. Not at the deadline — halfway. |
| **75%** | Escalate. Start finding alternates *now*, while there's still time. |
| **Due date** | Report **coverage by trade**. |

### The one number that matters: coverage

**Confirmed bidders per trade.**

| Coverage | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 🔴 0–1 | Uncovered. Your bid is exposed. Escalate today. |
| 🟠 2 | Thin. One drop-out and you're exposed. |
| 🟢 3+ | Covered. |

Two rules that prevent nearly every late-bid fire drill:

1. **"Sent" is not "confirmed."** Sending a package is not coverage. A trade with one
   unconfirmed bidder is an *uncovered* trade — and you want to know that on day 2,
   not day 9.
2. **Chase at 50%, never at the deadline.** Chasing on the due date means discovering
   the problem when it's too late to solve.

Your team should hand you **one number per project per week**: trades uncovered. That's
the whole report. Not a narrative.

## D. Presentations — build the kit once

For the top-tier GC/CM pursuits.

| Project | Tier | Needed by | Owner | Draft | Reviewed | Final |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

**Build a reusable library, once:**

- Firm overview + relevant project sheets by building type
- Safety record and EMR
- Key personnel bios (current headshots, current dates)
- Logistics and phasing approach templates
- Schedule and preconstruction methodology

Then each pursuit becomes **assembly + 20% customization**, not authorship from zero.

> You said you need "amazing presentations" for several projects. The way to get there
> consistently isn't more effort per deck — it's a strong library, so all your real time
> goes into the 20% that's specific to *this* client. That 20% is what wins. The other
> 80% just has to be clean, and it should never be written twice.
>
> **Only Tier 1 gets a custom deck.** That's what tiering is for.

1. **Sub solicitation and chasing** (section C) — fully theirs, protocol above
2. **Quantity takeoffs** against a standard template
3. **Bid-form assembly and compliance checks** — a checklist catches nearly everything
4. **Presentation assembly** from the kit — you write only the custom 20%

### What stays yours

Final number. Client relationships. Go/no-go calls. Tier 1 strategy.

### Monday, 20 minutes

Each person reports three things — **not a narrative**:

1. What's due this week
2. **Uncovered trades** (the number)
3. What they're blocked on

That's it. The meeting is for **unblocking**, not status collection. If it runs long,
you've drifted back into being the router.

> You chose to keep this system private for now, which is right for week one. But with
> five reports and 15+ pursuits, **you are the bottleneck by design** as long as every
> status passes through your head. The version that actually takes load off you is the
> one where they maintain their own rows. Do it once the rhythm is yours — but do it.

## F. Project folders

Standard structure, every project, no exceptions:

```
<Project Name>/
  01-RFP-and-Addenda/
  02-Drawings-and-Specs/
  03-Sub-Bids/           ← one subfolder per trade
  04-Pricing-Binder/
  05-Correspondence/
  06-Submission/
  07-Presentation/
```

Folders drift out of sync with email because filing is a *second* action after reading.
Two fixes that don't rely on discipline:

- **Save attachments while you're reading the mail.** Later never comes.
- **Put the project name in every subject line you control**, so search does the linking
  and the folder is a backup rather than the index.

### Move the binders out of personal Drive

- `Project # 2` → `Binder 24th Ave.pdf` (38 MB)
- `Project # 4` → `Binder North Facade and Porte Cochere.pdf` (40 MB)
- `193 Market St - Everest bids` — shared to your personal address by a sub

Sub pricing and pricing binders in a personal Google account is a real problem in a
dispute, an audit, or if you ever change firms. And `Project # 2` / `Project # 4` are
names that will mean nothing to you in six months.
