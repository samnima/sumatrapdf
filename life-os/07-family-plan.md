## The strategy, stated plainly

Education and living costs in Indonesia are a fraction of US costs — medical college
especially. On one salary, however high, four kids through US school and university is
not affordable. Jakarta is.

**This is a sound arbitrage, not indecision.** It stays in the system as a *plan*.

The sequence, however, is everything.

---

## ⚠️ The correction that changes the plan

> **There is no "educational green card." A green card does not permit living abroad
> to study.**

- More than **1 year** abroad without a re-entry permit → generally presumed abandoned
- Even **6+ months** can draw scrutiny at the border
- A **re-entry permit (Form I-131)** buys up to 2 years; renewals are scrutinized

A six-year medical program in Jakarta, held on a green card, would very likely end with
that green card gone — discovered years later, at an airport counter, with no remedy.

## ✅ The right order

> **Come here first → secure permanent status → *then* go abroad for cheap school.**

Once someone is a **US citizen**, living abroad costs them nothing. Citizenship cannot
be abandoned; a green card can. Get status permanent *first*, and Jakarta medical
college becomes pure upside — cheap education with nothing at risk. Exactly the path
your daughter already walked.

Reverse the order — stay in Jakarta now, immigrate later — and each child loses the
easy immigration category at 21, and you pay more for less.

### The clock

I-130 petitions: **filed, waiting.** Unmarried stepchildren under 21 sit in the fastest
category. At 21 they drop into a badly backlogged one.

| Child | Age | Turns 21 | Urgency |
|---|---|---|---|
| Eldest | **15** | ~2032 | 🔴 **Binding constraint** |
| | 13 | ~2034 | 🟠 |
| | 11 | ~2036 | 🟡 |
| | 9 | ~2038 | 🟢 |

Delay costs the 15-year-old most. Every month matters more for him than for the others.

### The wrinkle — they're stepchildren

Stepchildren generally do **not** automatically derive US citizenship through a
stepparent, the way biological or adopted children can. Possible routes: your wife
naturalizing while they are still minors, adoption, or each child naturalizing
individually after holding a green card.

**This is genuinely lawyer territory.** Not a spreadsheet problem.

## The single highest-value action available to you

> **Book one immigration attorney consultation. Bring the four I-130 receipt numbers.**

Higher value than the car decision, the inbox reset, or anything at work. Questions to
walk in with:

1. Given the petitions are filed and I'm the petitioner — what's each child's category,
   and what's the realistic timeline for each?
2. My 15-year-old ages out first. What protects him? Does CSPA apply here?
3. If they get green cards and then study in Indonesia — how do we avoid abandonment?
   Do re-entry permits actually cover a multi-year degree?
4. As **stepchildren**, what's each one's fastest route to citizenship? Does my wife
   naturalizing while they're minors change it? Does adoption?
5. What's the smartest **arrival order and timing** for wife and four kids?
