# 04 — Rhythm

Three loops. Daily (5 min), weekly (30 min), monthly (30 min). That's 4 hours a month
to never lose track of anything.

The weekly review is the one that actually matters. If you only ever do one thing from
this entire system, do the weekly review.

---

## Daily — 5 minutes, morning

Not a planning session. A scan.

1. **`2-WAITING` first.** Who owes you something that's overdue? Those are the loops
   that die silently — a sub who never sent pricing, a client who never answered.
   One nudge each. This step alone is most of "nothing falls through."
2. **Open Loops — anything due today or overdue.**
3. **Hub inbox — process to empty.** Not *read* to empty. Every message becomes
   `1-ACTION`, `2-WAITING`, `3-REF`, or deleted. Under 2 minutes → just do it now.
4. **Pick three.** Three things that would make today a win. Write them down. Three,
   not ten — ten is a wish list and you'll finish none of them.

## Weekly — 30 minutes, Friday afternoon or Sunday evening

**This is the missing half of your system.** You capture constantly and process never.
Everything downstream depends on this half hour existing.

Work the list in order:

1. **Drain `ME-DECIDE` to zero.** Every note becomes: Do (under 5 min), Date (goes to
   Open Loops), Decide (gets a forcing date), or Drop.
   > Expect to Drop 70%+. That's not waste — that's the point. Most of what you
   > capture is interesting, not important, and the cost of never sorting them is
   > that the important ones become invisible.
2. **Clear `ME-READ`.** Skim, keep what's genuinely useful, delete the rest. If a link
   has sat unread three weeks, you were never going to read it.
3. **Review every `WAITING` row.** Anything sitting more than a week gets a nudge or
   gets killed.
4. **Update the pursuit tracker** ([`05-pursuit-tracker.md`](05-pursuit-tracker.md))
   from your work inbox by hand — until IT connects Outlook, this is the only moment
   work enters the system. Don't skip it.
5. **Five employees, five lines.** One line each: what they're on, what's due, are they
   stuck. That's your Monday meeting agenda, written in five minutes.
6. **Look at the decisions list.** Any forcing date inside two weeks? Book the time now.

## Monthly — 30 minutes

- **Family budget vs actual** — school fees, expenses, the build. With your wife, in
  the shared sheet, so it doesn't depend on you both being awake at once.
- **Home build: what moved?** If nothing moved for a month, either kill it or schedule
  the next concrete step. A four-month-stalled project is worse than no project — it
  charges you attention and returns nothing.
- **Travel/points: what's booked, what needs booking.** Set your target route once.
  Then unsubscribe from every award-fare newsletter — you have 10+ and they're a
  slot machine, not a system.
- **Prune Open Loops.** Delete stale rows aggressively. A list you don't trust is worse
  than no list, because the moment you distrust it you go back to holding everything
  in your head — which is exactly where you are today.

---

## Why this order

Your instinct will be to start with the work stuff, because it's what stresses you
most. Resist that. Work is the one part that's **blocked on IT**, and it's also the part
where the system currently has the least visibility.

The daily/weekly rhythm is what makes the work tracker useful when it does arrive. If
you build the habit now on the personal side — where you have full control — then the
day Outlook connects, you drop RFPs into a machine that already runs.

Build the riverbed before you divert the biggest stream into it.
